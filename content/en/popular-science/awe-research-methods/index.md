This article systematically introduces the scientific definition of awe as an emotion in psychological research and the diverse research methods used to study it. The article first explains the concept of awe, noting that it is a complex emotional experience that arises when individuals face something vast that transcends their cognitive frameworks, and that it can be studied both as an emotional state and as a personality trait. It then describes in detail the various interesting research methods researchers have adopted: measuring awe traits and emotional experiences through questionnaires; using video materials such as the BBC documentary *Planet Earth* and music to induce awe experiences in the laboratory; priming participants' sense of awe through recall-writing and reading-writing tasks; and conducting field experiments in real settings, such as having participants look up at towering trees, climb high to overlook city scenery, or recruiting tourists at scenic spots to participate in research. The article notes that research on awe has found that this emotion can bring many positive effects, including enhancing the sense of time affluence, promoting charitable behavior, and improving environmental awareness and life satisfaction, demonstrating the important value and broad prospects of awe research.
