abstract: |
  Psychology aims to understand the human mind and behavior. In research practice, the extent to which the conclusions of scientific psychology can be generalized to all humans depends largely on whether its samples are representative of humanity. However, in global psychological science, most participants are undergraduates taking psychology courses, and most come from Western, Educated, Industrialized, Rich, and Democratic countries or regions—characteristics known as "WEIRD" (Henrich et al., 2010). The lack of diversity and representativeness of participants, on the one hand, limits psychology's complete depiction of the human mind and behavior, and on the other hand, affects the generalizability of psychological research. To address this issue, many researchers have called for and participated in large-scale international collaborative projects, hoping to collect more samples and data from non-WEIRD regions. Large-scale international collaborative projects have indeed increased the geographic diversity and number of samples. However, it remains unknown whether participants from non-WEIRD regions can represent the local populations of the surveyed regions. To understand this issue, this study sampled 1,000 empirical studies from papers published in five mainstream Chinese academic journals—*Acta Psychologica Sinica*, *Journal of Psychological Science*, *Chinese Journal of Clinical Psychology*, *Psychological Development and Education*, and *Studies of Psychology and Behavior*—across three different periods (2008, 2017–2018, and 2020–2021), and extracted participant information. At the same time, the characteristics of Chinese participants reported in large-scale international collaborative projects (e.g., Many Labs projects, the Human Penguin Project, PSA) were extracted, in order to understand the current status of participant samples in empirical psychological research and compare them with data from China's seventh national population census. The results of this study provide a realistic picture of Chinese psychology participants, and the study also discusses potential solutions to representativeness problems in China and globally.
