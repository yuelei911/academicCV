abstract: |
  Recent years have repeatedly seen phenomena such as occupying others' high-speed rail seats, staged traffic accidents, and deadbeat debtors, each time sparking heated discussion across society and causing many negative effects. Unethical behavior refers to illegal or morally unacceptable behavior, including violence, non-violent crime, lawbreaking, deception, and many other behaviors. Previous research has found that materialism is an important influencing factor of unethical behavior, but most prior studies used questionnaire methods to explore their relationship, lacking diverse perspectives to verify it. This study examines the effect of materialism on unethical behavior from three perspectives—big data, questionnaires, and experiments—across four studies, aiming to enrich the perspectives on their relationship and enhance the reliability of the conclusions. Study 1 used the sixth World Values Survey with 89,565 samples and, after controlling for income, age, and gender, found that materialism significantly positively predicted various unethical behaviors such as deception, theft, bribery, domestic violence, and violence against others. Study 2a used the Materialistic Values Scale and the Honesty-Humility subscale to survey 245 participants in Sichuan, Shanghai, and Liaoning; after controlling for gender, age, and subjective social class, materialism positively predicted individuals' dishonest behavior. To increase sample representativeness and improve the reliability of conclusions, Study 2b used the Materialistic Values Scale, the Unethical Decision-Making Scale, and the Moral Foundations Sacredness Scale to survey 342 participants in Zhejiang, Hunan, and Shaanxi; after controlling for gender, age, and subjective social class, materialism positively predicted individuals' unethical behavior in social life situations. Study 3 used an experimental method to explore the causal relationship between materialism and unethical behavior, priming materialism through an imagination paradigm and measuring unethical behavior with an impossible task; the results showed that the materialism priming group exhibited more unethical behavior than the control group. This study confirms from multiple perspectives the effect of materialism on unethical behavior, enriching the research approaches to their relationship and suggesting that interventions for unethical behavior can start with materialistic values.
