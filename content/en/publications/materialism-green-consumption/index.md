abstract: |
  In contemporary Chinese society, where materialistic values are prevalent, how to promote green consumption is a question worthy of attention. Previous studies have reached contradictory conclusions regarding the relationship between materialism and green consumption. To address this situation, this study takes the characteristics of green products themselves (conspicuous/non-conspicuous) as its starting point. It first examines the boundary condition of materialism's influence on green consumption—namely, the moderating role of the conspicuousness of green products—and then further explores the psychological mechanisms by which materialism promotes conspicuous green consumption and inhibits non-conspicuous green consumption. Finally, on this basis, an intervention study involving nature contact is conducted, aiming to provide new insights for resolving existing research discrepancies and to offer management recommendations for enterprises and governments in guiding green consumption.
