abstract: |
  Objective: To examine the effect of materialism on college students' relational self-esteem and the mediating role of perceived social support. Methods: A total of 419 college students were surveyed using the Materialistic Values Scale, the Perceived Social Support Scale, and the Relational Self-Esteem Scale. Results: (1) Correlation analysis showed that materialism was significantly negatively correlated with perceived social support (r = -0.19, p < 0.001) and relational self-esteem (r = -0.13, p < 0.01), while perceived social support was significantly positively correlated with relational self-esteem (r = 0.59, p < 0.001); (2) Mediation model testing showed that perceived social support significantly mediated the relationship between materialism and relational self-esteem (95% CI = [-0.18, -0.04]). Conclusion: Materialism negatively affects college students' relational self-esteem through perceived social support.
